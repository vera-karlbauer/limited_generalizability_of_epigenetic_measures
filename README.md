## Abstract
Epigenetic clocks and scores have been investigated as potential biomarkers of later-life health outcomes following 
early-life adversity (ELA). In pediatric settings, DNA methylation (DNAm) is often measured in saliva; however, most 
clocks and scores have been trained in adult blood. In the Kids2Health cohort, we leveraged matched blood and saliva 
from 291 children (3-12 years, 56% exposed to ELA) to systematically evaluate 22 epigenetic measures for phenotypic 
validity, cross-tissue correlation, longitudinal stability, and sensitivity to adversity. Most measures tracked their 
target phenotypes across tissues, however, epigenetic age acceleration estimates showed only low to moderate blood-saliva 
correlations (r = 0.20–0.68), Nonetheless, epigenetic scores for cognitive ability and inflammation were robustly 
associated with ELA in both tissues, and biological epigenetic clocks were associated with low SES and linked to 
cardiometabolic biomarkers in blood and saliva, supporting their utility as indicators of health risk even in childhood. 
In addition to a resource of epigenome-wide blood-saliva correlations for pediatric biomarker development, we provide 
recommendations for the interpretation and application of epigenetic biomarkers in children.

## Data availability
Due to the sensitive nature of the data and the informed consent agreements with participants and parents, the individual-level Kids2Health data are not publicly available. 
Interested researchers can obtain a deidentified dataset after approval from the study board (christine.heim@charite.de). 
Clock- and score-based analysis results (*Supplementary Tables 1-13*) are available as Supplementary Data of the corresponding publication.  
Epigenome-wide associations (*Supplementary Table 14*) are available on Zenodo: [https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17899483](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17899483)
